# Front-page reading notes and information gain

This file records what was actually read from the visible front results and direct destination pages. It does not select the final intent and does not provide article copy.

## Result-family reading

### Item and mechanics pages

The canonical Speed-Gro page, its `stardew.wiki` mirror, Deluxe Speed-Gro, Hyper Speed-Gro, and the compact `stardewvalley.tools` recipe page dominate the direct lookup variants. They cover the item’s effect tier, recipe/unlock, acquisition, price, and basic application boundary. The canonical wiki page also exposes the important late-application/stage caveat and a game-code reference.

Information still missing when these pages are read alone: a reader cannot easily decide whether a saved day changes the harvest count for a particular planting date, and the pages do not by themselves explain a cost-versus-harvest decision.

### Official 1.6 changelog

The first-party changelog is not a Speed-Gro guide. It is valuable because it supplies exact version-change wording: regular Speed-Gro moved from Clam to Moss, and Deluxe Speed-Gro moved from Coral to five bone fragments. This is the strongest way to prevent old recipe instructions from leaking into a current guide.

### Decision and ROI pages

The StardewProfit guide is structured around “When Is Fertilizer Worth the Cost?” and explicitly includes a formula, comparison table, Starfruit and Parsnip examples, late-season planting, Ancient Fruit, Agriculturist, and FAQ questions. It demonstrates clear demand for a decision guide, but its calculations are author analysis and are not promoted to facts here.

The Steam guide frames Speed-Gro versus Basic Fertilizer as an opportunity-cost decision. It discusses one fertilizer per tile, growth-cycle rounding, fast-crop examples, early/mid-game access, and pros/cons. It explicitly acknowledges a lack of hard statistics, so it is useful for language and information architecture, not authority.

### Community question

The Reddit thread is an especially useful reader-language source: the author asks whether `0.8` of an eight-day crop can exist and whether Speed-Gro “takes a day off.” Comments move to Blueberries, Cranberries, Strawberries, crop-calendar references, and regrow timing. The thread is old and archived, so its value is diagnostic: it reveals the exact confusion a current mechanics explanation should resolve.

### Calculator and calendar pages

The CodingAce calculator makes the implied task explicit: base growth days, season days left, planting day, costs, sale price, plots, yield, regrow days, fertilizer tier, replant choice, and Agriculturist. The Crop Growth Calendars page gives the source-backed crop-by-crop tables that a calculator-oriented article needs. The information gain is to connect those two jobs while clearly labelling any calculation as an example and validating the mechanics against the current source page.

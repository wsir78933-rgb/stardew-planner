Stand next to a ripe parsnip and right-click it. The March 26, 2019 reply on the Steam thread [Harvest Parsnip with Scythe??](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/1849197902668085831/) says a parsnip does not need a scythe: stand beside it and right-click. [Kale](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Kale&oldid=192520), [amaranth](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Amaranth&oldid=192043), [wheat](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Wheat&oldid=192731), [unmilled rice](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Unmilled_Rice&oldid=194130), and [fiber seeds](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Fiber_Seeds&oldid=193587) are cut with a scythe.

## How to Harvest Standard Crops by Hand

### Default PC Controls: Right-Click and the Check/Do Action

On PC, that parsnip pick is Right-Click. The [Controls](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Controls&oldid=192481) page names the same physical inputs: Right-Click or X is Check/Do Action, and Left-Click or C is Use tool or place item. Every hotkey on that page can be reassigned in Options, so the labels below are the defaults.

The growth time is on the [Parsnip](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Parsnip&oldid=191123) page. The right-click itself is the Steam reply. X is the same click in the forum post quoted in the next section. The Steam thread is from 2019, before the 1.6 Iridium Scythe.

Left-Click is also how you place seeds and fertilizer. The [Crops](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Crops&oldid=193979) page says to put them on a tilled tile with the left-click control, or across tiles by holding Right-Click or Left-Click. Planting and fertilizing need no tool and spend no energy. That paragraph is about putting seeds and fertilizer down. The parsnip pick is the right-click from the Steam reply.

Controller names for Check / Do Action and Use tool:

- Xbox overworld, on an Xbox One or Xbox 360 controller: A is Check / Do Action / Use Cursor, and X is Use Tool. Controller buttons cannot be reassigned in the game.
- PlayStation overworld: Cross is Check / Do Action / Use Cursor, and Square is Use Tool. A system remap lives under Settings, Accessibility, Button Assignments.
- Switch overworld: A is Check / Do Action / Use Cursor, and Y is Use Tool. The system remap is under System Settings, Controllers and Sensors, Change Button Mapping. A later Weapons block on the same page uses Y for a basic attack and A for block or alternate attack. That pair is the weapons pair.

Switch 2 mouse controls, while they are turned on, use R to pick up furniture or use a tool, and ZR to rotate furniture, interact with the environment, or use other items. The right stick scrolls the toolbar. You can turn mouse controls off in Options.

On mobile, the default scheme is Tap-to-move and Auto-Attack. Tapping stone or wood can select a tool for you. The [Mobile Controls](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Mobile_Controls&oldid=189843) page names one exception: the scythe has to be selected. Other mobile schemes map Button A to perform action, called right-click on PC, and Button X to use tool, called left-click on PC.

### Holding Right-Click on the Tile You Stand On

Elenna101, on the forum thread [PC controls vs mobile](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/pc-controls-vs-mobile.8990/), which started on September 17, 2021, described a hold for the tile under your character. The sentence is: "If you hold right click with the mouse not near your character, you'll automatically plant seed and harvest on the tile you're standing on." The same post says X is automatically mapped to right-click.

Keep the mouse off your character while you hold. The hold harvests the tile you are standing on. If seeds are in your hand, that same hold also plants that tile. A 1.4 note on the Controls page is a different hold: it makes it easier to collect from a lot of kegs, preserves jars, and crystalariums. Those are machines. The crop hold is the forum sentence.

## Crops That Require a Scythe to Harvest

### The Scythe-Harvested Crops in Stardew Valley

You start with one [Scythe](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Scythe&oldid=171696). A swing spends no energy. It cuts grass, weeds, young trees, and moss, and it can be swung as a weak weapon. The blacksmith does not upgrade it. From 1.6, dying no longer removes the starter Scythe, or the Golden Scythe, from your inventory.

The scythe page gives amaranth, kale, and wheat as examples. The harvest line on each of these pages is:

- Kale grows in spring and takes 6 days. Kale is harvested using a Scythe or better.
- Amaranth grows in fall and takes 7 days. Amaranth is harvested using a Scythe or better.
- Wheat grows in summer or fall and takes 4 days. Wheat is harvested using a Scythe or better. A harvest with any scythe also has a 40 percent chance to drop hay. That chance is on the wheat harvest. Hay from cutting grass is a separate rate on the tool pages.
- Unmilled rice grows in spring from rice shoots and is harvested using a Scythe. Most tiles take 8 days. Shoots within about 3 tiles of water are irrigated, mature in 6 days, and do not need watering.
- Fiber seeds say to harvest with the scythe. They take 7 days, grow in any season including winter, and do not need daily watering. They still need tilled soil. Each plant drops 4 to 7 Fiber, with a 10 percent chance of one Mixed Seeds. The Fiber Seeds page says they are not considered Crops.

On PC, select the scythe and swing it with Use tool: Left-Click or C. On mobile, select the scythe yourself.

### Comparing Scythes: Starter Scythe, Golden Scythe, and the Iridium Scythe

The [Golden Scythe](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Golden_Scythe&oldid=191051) comes from the statue in the Quarry Mine. It was introduced in 1.4. Compared with the starter scythe, it has a larger radius, a higher chance to cut all the grass in that radius, a 75 percent chance per swipe to harvest hay from grass, and 13 damage instead of 1 to 3.

The [Iridium Scythe](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Iridium_Scythe&oldid=186836) was introduced in 1.6. You receive it after claiming Farming Mastery. The [Mastery Cave](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Mastery_Cave&oldid=191046) opens after you reach level 10 in all five skills. Mastery points accumulate only after that, Farming experience counts at 50 percent toward those points, and you choose the order of the rewards. Farming level 10 on its own is not the unlock. Its text says, "Can be used to harvest any crops." It harvests items grown on tilled soil within the swing, including regular crops, crops in Garden Pots, Spring Onions, and forageables grown from Wild Seeds. The radius is larger than the Golden Scythe. Hay from grass is 100 percent per swipe, and the damage is 20 instead of 13. On the [wiki front page](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/) read on October 12, 2026, the computer row was labeled 1.6.15, and the PlayStation Vita row was labeled 1.02. This 1.6 scythe sits outside that Vita label.

In 1.6.9, the Iridium Scythe history says harvesting crops grown from Wild Seeds was fixed so they no longer always produced normal quality and gave no XP or Mastery. The [1.6.9 changelog](https://www.stardewvalley.net/stardew-valley-1-6-9-changelog/) says the Gatherer chance to double harvested forage also applies when you use an iridium scythe, forage crops from garden pots no longer always come out low quality with no XP, and an iridium scythe on forage crops gives XP and the forage-level quality boost.

On PC, a parsnip uses Right-Click or X. Kale, wheat, amaranth, unmilled rice, and fiber seeds use a scythe, and that swing is Left-Click or C.

## How to Tell When Your Crops Are Ready to Harvest

### Calculating Growth Days and the Watering Requirement

Growth times on the Crops page leave out the day you plant. A 5-day crop planted on day 1 is ready on day 6, not day 5. The Parsnip page lists 4 days, and the stage days add up to 4. [Getting Started](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Getting_Started&oldid=195315) says parsnips are ready to harvest in 4 days. The same count, applied to that 4-day listing, is ready on day 5 when you plant on day 1.

An unwatered day does not advance growth. Each dry day pushes the ready day back by one.

### Single-Harvest vs. Regrowing Crops

A few crops keep producing after the first pick.

- Green Bean uses a trellis and continues to produce after maturity. Regrowth is 3 days.
- Strawberry gives 1 strawberry every 4 days. The table lists regrowth of 4 days.
- Coffee continues to produce after maturity.

The parsnip listing is a 4-day growth time. It has no regrowth interval next to it.

## What Happens If You Leave Crops Unharvested?

### Unharvested Crops Within the Active Season

The wither rule on the Crops page is the season change. After day 28, a crop that is no longer in season withers and dies. A ripe crop left until a later morning of the same season has not reached that change. A crop that is already fully grown and ready still withers when the next season is one it cannot occupy.

### Season Rollover: When Outdoor Crops Wilt

On the 1st, crops that are no longer in season are dead plants, and the soil stays tilled. Clear the dead plants with a scythe. That swing removes a dead crop. It is a different job from cutting ripe kale or wheat.

Crops that are still in season continue. The named multi-season crops are Ancient Fruit, Coffee Bean, Corn, Sunflower, and Wheat. A wheat plant of any growth stage that is in the ground on Summer 28 continues on Fall 1, and the fertilizer under it remains. Fully grown wheat can stay until Fall 1 when you want to keep that fertilizer and the tilled soil. Cutting it during summer is a different choice.

When the first harvest has to happen before that morning, the last planting day is counted in [Last Day to Plant in Stardew Valley](/last-day-to-plant-stardew).

After the [Greenhouse](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/index.php?title=Greenhouse&oldid=194164) is repaired, crops there can be planted, grown, and harvested at any time of year. They still need water, including on rainy days. Crops that regrow do not die at the end of a season. Multiple-harvest crops and fertilizer stay until you remove them. Giant crops cannot grow in the greenhouse. The same page says fertilizer persistence is also true on Ginger Island. Choosing what to plant in that room is covered in [What to Grow in Greenhouse Stardew](/what-to-grow-in-greenhouse-stardew).

## Special Harvesting Rules: Giant Crops and Trellises

### Harvesting 3×3 Giant Crops with an Axe

Cauliflower, Melon, Pumpkin, Powdermelon, and Qi Fruit in a 3×3 can become a giant crop. The current condition is that the top-left crop is fully grown and watered. Harvest with any axe. It takes three hits and drops 15 to 21 normal-quality items. A giant crop does not die at a season change. Giant crops cannot grow in the greenhouse, in Garden Pots, or on Ginger Island.

### Navigating Impassable Trellis Crops

Green Bean, Hops, and Grape need a trellis. You cannot walk through that tile until the crop is dead, so leave a tile beside each plant where you can stand to pick it. Place the rows on the [Stardew Valley Farm Planner](https://stardewvalleyplanner.art) and keep a standing tile beside each trellis. The planner places crops and checks coverage.

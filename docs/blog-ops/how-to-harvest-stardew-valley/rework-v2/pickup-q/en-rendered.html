How to Harvest in Stardew Valley: Right-Click a Parsnip; Kale Needs a Scythe

Stand beside a ripe parsnip and right-click or press X. Kale, amaranth, wheat, unmilled rice, and fiber seeds come off with a scythe swing. Count growth from the day after planting, and a dry day moves the ready day back.

By Stardew Valley Planner Team · 7 min read

Stand next to a ripe parsnip and right-click it. A parsnip does not need a scythe. Kale, amaranth, wheat, unmilled rice, and fiber seeds are cut with a scythe.

How to Harvest Standard Crops by Hand

Default PC Controls: Right-Click and the Check/Do Action

On PC, stand on a tile beside the ripe parsnip and press Right-Click. One click harvests it. X is mapped to the same click, so X harvests it too.

The default PC controls bind Right-Click or X to Check / Do Action, and Left-Click or C to Use tool or place item. You can reassign every hotkey in Options. The names here are the defaults.

Left-Click puts seeds and fertilizer on a tilled tile. One click covers one tile. Hold Right-Click or Left-Click and move when you want several tiles. Planting and fertilizing need no tool and spend no energy. Picking the ripe parsnip stays on Right-Click or X.

Controller overworld buttons use other names for those two commands:

  • Xbox, on an Xbox One or Xbox 360 controller: A is Check / Do Action / Use Cursor, and X is Use Tool. Controller buttons cannot be reassigned in the game.
  • PlayStation: Cross is Check / Do Action / Use Cursor, and Square is Use Tool.
  • Switch: A is Check / Do Action / Use Cursor, and Y is Use Tool.

Continuous Harvesting: The Hold-and-Walk Method

For more than one plant, hold Right-Click or X and keep the mouse away from your character. That hold plants seeds and harvests on the tile you are standing on. If seeds are in your hand, the same hold plants that tile while it harvests. Then walk around over your crops. The hold keeps using the tile under your feet.

Holding a button to collect from many kegs, preserves jars, or crystalariums is a 1.4 shortcut for those machines. For crops, keep the mouse away from your character so the hold stays on the tile under your feet.

Crops That Require a Scythe to Harvest

The Scythe-Harvested Crops in Stardew Valley

You start with one Scythe. A swing spends no energy. It cuts grass, weeds, young trees, and moss, and you can swing it as a weak weapon. The blacksmith does not upgrade it. From 1.6, dying no longer removes the starter Scythe or the Golden Scythe from your inventory.

On PC, put the scythe in your hand and swing with Use tool: Left-Click or C. On mobile, select the scythe yourself before that swing.

Amaranth, kale, and wheat are harvested with a scythe or better. Unmilled rice and fiber seeds come off with a scythe swing as well.

  • Kale grows in spring and takes 6 days.
  • Amaranth is a fall crop and takes 7 days.
  • Wheat grows in summer or fall and takes 4 days. A harvest with any scythe also has a 40 percent chance to drop hay.
  • Unmilled rice grows in spring from rice shoots and is harvested with a scythe. Most tiles take 8 days. Shoots within about 3 tiles of water are irrigated, mature in 6 days, and do not need watering. Plant those shoots near water when you want the shorter wait.
  • Fiber seeds are harvested with the scythe. They are not crops. They take 7 days, grow in any season including winter, and do not need daily watering. They still need tilled soil. Each plant drops 4 to 7 Fiber, with a 10 percent chance of one Mixed Seeds.

Comparing Scythes: Starter Scythe, Golden Scythe, and the Iridium Scythe

The Golden Scythe comes from the statue in the Quarry Mine. It was introduced in 1.4. Next to the starter scythe, it has a larger radius, a higher chance to cut all the grass in that radius, a 75 percent chance per swipe to harvest hay from grass, and 13 damage instead of 1 to 3.

The Iridium Scythe was introduced in 1.6. You receive it after claiming Farming Mastery. It can be used to harvest any crops. The swing collects items grown on tilled soil within its range, including regular crops, crops in garden pots, spring onions, and forageables grown from wild seeds. The radius is larger than the Golden Scythe. Hay from grass is 100 percent per swipe, and the damage is 20 instead of 13.

In 1.6.9, the Gatherer chance to double harvested forage also applies when you use an iridium scythe. Forage crops from garden pots no longer always come out low quality with no experience. An iridium scythe on forage crops gives experience and the forage-level quality boost.

How to Tell When Your Crops Are Ready to Harvest

Calculating Growth Days and the Watering Requirement

Growth time leaves out the day you plant. A 5-day crop planted on day 1 is ready on day 6. A parsnip takes 4 days. Plant one on Spring 1 and water it every day: it is ready on Spring 5. Right-click it that day.

An unwatered day does not advance growth. Each dry day pushes the ready day back by one. Miss the watering on Spring 3 and water the other days, and that parsnip is ready on Spring 6.

Use the same count on the scythe crops. Kale planted and watered on Spring 1 is ready on Spring 7. Amaranth planted on Fall 1 is ready on Fall 8. Wheat planted on Summer 1 is ready on Summer 5. Unmilled rice planted on Spring 1 is ready on Spring 9 on ordinary soil, and on Spring 7 when the shoots sit within about 3 tiles of water. Fiber seeds planted on day 1 are ready on day 8.

Single-Harvest vs. Regrowing Crops

A few crops keep producing after the first pick.

  • Green bean uses a trellis and continues to produce after maturity. Regrowth is 3 days, so you come back to the same plant rather than sowing that spot again.
  • Strawberry gives 1 strawberry every 4 days.
  • Coffee continues to produce after maturity.

A parsnip gives you that one harvest. Pick it, and it does not produce again the way a green bean or a strawberry does.

What Happens If You Leave Crops Unharvested?

Unharvested Crops Within the Active Season

A ripe crop can stay in the ground overnight while its season is still going. A ready parsnip left for another day in that same season is still there to pick. After day 28, a crop that is no longer in season withers and dies. A crop that is already fully grown still dies when the next season is one it cannot occupy.

If the first harvest has to happen before the season changes, count the last safe planting day in Last Day to Plant in Stardew Valley.

Season Rollover: When Outdoor Crops Wilt

On the 1st, crops that are no longer in season are dead plants, and the soil stays tilled. Clear those dead plants with a scythe. Cut a living scythe crop while it is still in season if you want the harvest.

Crops that are still in season continue. The multi-season crops are Ancient Fruit, Coffee Bean, Corn, Sunflower, and Wheat. A wheat plant of any growth stage that is in the ground on Summer 28 continues on Fall 1, and the fertilizer under it remains. Leave fully grown wheat until Fall 1 when you want that fertilizer and the tilled soil to carry into fall. Harvest it during summer when you want the grain and the 40 percent hay chance before fall.

After the Greenhouse is repaired, crops there can be planted, grown, and harvested at any time of year. They still need water, including on rainy days. Crops that regrow do not die at the end of a season. Multiple-harvest crops and fertilizer stay until you remove them. Giant crops cannot grow in the greenhouse. Choosing what to plant in that room is covered in What to Grow in Greenhouse Stardew.

Special Harvesting Rules: Giant Crops and Trellises

Harvesting 3×3 Giant Crops with an Axe

Cauliflower, Melon, Pumpkin, Powdermelon, and Qi Fruit in a 3×3 can become a giant crop. It can form when the top-left crop is fully grown and watered. Harvest with any axe. It takes three hits and drops 15 to 21 normal-quality items. A giant crop does not die at a season change, so those three hits can wait. Giant crops cannot grow in the greenhouse, in garden pots, or on Ginger Island.

Navigating Impassable Trellis Crops

Green bean, hops, and grape need a trellis. You cannot walk through that tile until the crop is dead, so leave a tile beside each plant where you can stand to pick it. Green beans need that standing tile again every 3 days after the first harvest. Hops and grapes block the same way for as long as the plant is alive.

Place the rows on the Stardew Valley Farm Planner and keep a standing tile beside each trellis. The planner places crops and checks coverage.
